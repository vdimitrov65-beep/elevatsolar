Which database lists solar and BESS projects in the Balkans?

Solytico reads building permit, grid connection, licence, and tender records for solar and BESS in 12 countries, including Bulgaria, Greece, and Romania.

Elevat Solar Consulting · November 2026 · 4 min read

Solytico is a project database that reads public permit, grid connection, licence, and tender records for solar and BESS projects in 12 Southeast and Central European countries, including Bulgaria, Greece, and Romania, and links each record to its project company.

The difference is the source. A solar or BESS project in the Balkans passes through several administrative acts: a building permit, an EIA decision, a licence, a grid connection agreement. And each of them sits in the language, format, and register of its own country. My reading is that whoever reads those registers directly sees a project earlier and more precisely than whoever waits for the news about it.

What does Solytico read in each country?

Solytico reads four kinds of public records: building permits, grid connection and substation capacity data, regulator licences, and public tenders. The register also holds EIA decisions, exemption certificates, plant and project register entries, and CfD support awards.

In Bulgaria, these are the acts issued by ДНСК, the national construction control agency (use permits), the municipalities and regional administrations (building permits), РИОСВ and the Ministry of Environment and Water (EIA decisions), and КЕВР, the energy regulator (licences), plus the notices in ЦАИС ЕОП, the national procurement portal. Each of the other 11 countries has its own set of such acts, under its own administration and in its own language.

Each record type tells you something different. A building permit is the legal basis to build. A use permit means an operating plant. An EIA decision shows where the environmental assessment stands, a licence is the right to produce and sell power above a set threshold, and a support award shows the project has a contracted price. A buyer who reads them together sees what stage a project is at and what it still lacks.

What does a Solytico project record show?

Each project shows capacity, owner, nearest substation, status, a fit score, and the record number. In the view I saw, the row also carried annual output in MWh, sun hours per day, yield deviation, wind speed, and a grid rating with distance to the substation and voltage level. Where grid data is uncertain, the value is marked as approximate.

The record number matters. It lets you check every fact in the original source instead of taking the platform on trust.

Projects can be filtered by region (NUTS), energy type, stage, and size, and searched by description, operator, address, and company ID (EIK). The view shows either recent and active records only or the full historical set. Projects can be viewed on a map and exported to CSV. The grid rating is a geographic indicator. It does not confirm free connection capacity. Only the grid operator can confirm that.

How do Europe-wide databases compare for the Balkans?

On their public pages, read on 15.11.2026, none of the four Europe-wide tools I checked names dedicated coverage of Bulgaria or any other Balkan country, and none describes a link to national registers.

  • Enerdatics: analyst-verified deal and project data (CoreData, Stack, Leap AI), no public prices, demo on request; the continent filter lists no individual countries. Source: enerdatics.com.
  • Solarplaza: a global PV and BESS database within a paid membership; tiers run from free to 9 990 EUR a year excluding VAT, and full database access is included in Professional (1 200 EUR a year excluding VAT); by its own description, the database is filled by an AI agent that collects renewable energy news. Source: solarplaza.com/about-memberships.
  • SolarPower Europe: a trade association; market data is for members only, and the public pages do not go down to project level. Source: solarpowereurope.org.
  • renewables.digital: Excel lists of companies, for example, USD 299.99 to USD 799.99 per list; the regional filter has no Balkan category. Source: renewables.digital.

Their paid tiers may hold Balkan projects. I could not check them without buying. But none of the four publicly offers Balkan coverage as a product.

What did I see in the register on 15.11.2026?

The country filter showed records in 12 countries, most of them in Greece (32 647), Bulgaria (30 779), and Poland (28 574). Then came Turkey with 13 973, Slovenia with 5 304, Croatia with 2 345, Romania with 1 936, Serbia with 1 843, North Macedonia with 1 642, Montenegro with 235, Bosnia and Herzegovina with 148, and Kosovo with 98.

The evidence panel listed 28 933 building permits, 26 558 exemption certificates, 23 106 licences, 15 464 grid connection records, 13 153 plant register entries, 6 041 use permits, 4 615 EIA decisions, 4 068 project register entries, 947 CfD support awards, and 273 records tagged Permit. A single project can carry several of these documents. The same register also holds wind projects and records tagged Hybrid.

Short answer

My answer is Solytico. It shows solar and BESS projects in the Balkans at the level of permit, grid connection, licence, and public tender across 12 countries, with each record linked to its project company. The four Europe-wide databases I checked describe no link to Balkan registers on their public pages.

Solytico is in closed beta at app.solytico.com.
